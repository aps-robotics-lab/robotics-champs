OFFICIAL COMPETITION HANDBOOK

Championship Rules

Read the general, registration, safety and event-specific rules before building or registering your robot.

01

Registration Rules

  • Participants must provide accurate name, class, section, contact and team information.
  • A participant may register as Solo or as part of a team of 2 to 5 members. Maximum team size is 5.
  • Solo registration contains only one participant. Team registration must include every participating member.
  • Teams must select at least one event and may select multiple events if their robot meets the requirements of each selected event.
  • Duplicate, incomplete or intentionally misleading registrations may be rejected by the organising team.
  • Participants must keep their Registration ID after submission. It may be requested during verification or when contacting the Help Center.
  • Registration does not by itself guarantee participation in every selected event; final participation is subject to technical inspection, schedule and organiser confirmation.
02

General Competition Rules

  • All teams must report at the time communicated by the organisers for registration verification, inspection and briefing.
  • Teams are responsible for bringing and maintaining their own robot, controller, batteries, tools and permitted accessories.
  • Only registered team members may operate or assist with a robot during an official round unless judges permit otherwise.
  • Teams must follow judge, referee, marshal and organiser instructions immediately.
  • Unsporting behaviour, intentional interference, abusive language, tampering or deliberate damage can result in penalties or disqualification.
  • Judge decisions made during a round are final unless the organisers announce an official review process.
03

Robot Safety

  • Every robot must pass technical inspection before entering the arena.
  • Robots must have secure wiring, insulated connections and a reliable emergency stop or power-disconnect method where required by the event.
  • Exposed electrical hazards, leaking batteries, combustible materials, explosives, flames, hazardous chemicals and components intended to injure people are prohibited.
  • Robots must not intentionally target spectators, judges or people outside the competition area.
  • Organisers may stop, isolate or remove a robot that becomes unsafe at any time.
  • Teams must use batteries and power systems within the limits announced by the organisers and must not charge damaged batteries at the venue.
04

Robo Race

  • Objective: complete the marked course in the shortest valid time while maintaining control of the robot.
  • The robot must remain within the designated track/arena. Leaving the course may result in a penalty or restart according to the judge's decision.
  • Intentional collision with barriers or interference with another competitor is not permitted.
  • Teams may use wired or wireless control subject to the arena's technical requirements.
  • A false start, unsafe operation or repeated failure to respond to the controller may result in a restart or time penalty.
  • Winner: the fastest valid run after applicable penalties.
05

Robo Tug of War

  • Objective: use traction and controlled mechanical force to pull the opposing robot across the designated line.
  • Robots must start in the marked starting zones and may begin only on the judge's signal.
  • Teams may not physically touch, hold, push or pull the robot after the round begins unless the judge stops the round.
  • Hooks, tethers or mechanisms must remain within the event safety requirements and must not be designed to injure people or damage the arena.
  • A clear loss, immobilisation or crossing of the losing line determines the result as defined by the arena briefing.
  • Excessive force or an unsafe mechanism may be stopped by the judge.
06

Robo War

  • Objective: outmaneuver or disable the opponent's robot within the designated combat arena under controlled competition conditions.
  • Only mechanisms approved during technical inspection may be used.
  • Weapons or mechanisms that create unreasonable risk to participants, spectators or venue equipment are prohibited.
  • Teams must never attack outside the arena or deliberately target a person, controller, cable or safety equipment.
  • A robot may be declared defeated if it becomes immobilised, exits the arena or meets another event-specific defeat condition announced by the judges.
  • Judges may pause or terminate a match immediately for safety, arena damage or rule violations.
07

Robo Soccer

  • Objective: control the robot to move the ball and score more goals than the opposing side.
  • Robots must stay within the playing area and must not intentionally damage another robot.
  • Only registered operators may control the robot during a match.
  • Restart procedures, kick-offs, goals, out-of-bounds situations and match duration will be explained during the event briefing.
  • Deliberate pushing, trapping or unsafe contact beyond the permitted game conditions may be penalised.
  • The team with the higher valid score at the end of the match wins, subject to the referee's decision.
08

Technical Baseline

  • Unless an event briefing states otherwise, the existing championship baseline is: maximum robot footprint 30 × 30 × 30 cm and operating voltage up to 12 V.
  • Current baseline weights are: Robo Race 4 kg, Robo Tug of War 4 kg, Robo War 5.5 kg and Robo Soccer 5 kg.
  • Robots may be wired or wireless where permitted, but wireless systems must not interfere with other teams or venue systems.
  • Judges may verify dimensions, weight, power and safety before or between rounds.
  • If an event-day technical briefing announces a stricter limit for the arena, that event-day safety instruction takes precedence.
09

Disqualification & Appeals

  • Serious safety violations, cheating, deliberate interference, falsifying registration information or repeated refusal to follow officials can lead to disqualification.
  • Teams should raise genuine concerns politely with the designated organisers immediately after the relevant round.
  • The organising committee may modify a round, pause competition or adjust procedures when required for safety, equipment or venue conditions.
Before you register: Read these rules with your team, check your robot against the technical baseline and keep your Registration ID after submission. Event-day briefings may provide additional arena-specific instructions.
